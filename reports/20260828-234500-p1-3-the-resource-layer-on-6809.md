### 1 — Summary

`tools/volread/` is ported to the 6809. The four DIR tables are resident and parsed on target; a caller
asks for `(type, index)` and receives bytes; the payload is pulled through a single 8 KB MMU window that
is remapped as the record crosses block boundaries; and resources are held resident in a 12 KB arena
under a **stack** policy chosen from the measured LOGIC working set rather than from preference.

**1,264 resources across 3 titles and 10 volumes are byte-identical to the oracle**, spanning all four
types. **1,560 DIR slots** — present and empty alike — parse identically to `tools/volread/` on target.
**The gate is shown still capable of failing** at exactly the corrupted resource, twice by injection and
four more times on dangling entries the corpus supplied on its own. **124 LOGIC resources are `Avis
Durgan`-decoded on the 6809**, 1,616 messages, digest-identical to the oracle with the bytecode section
provably untouched.

**AC-7's number, which the dispatch flagged as the one the VM will be planned against: 1,022–1,029
cycles fixed plus 11.98 cycles per byte, at a calibrated 0.8937 MHz, with an MMU remap costing 263–300
cycles and occurring 0.26 times per fetch.** ★★ **The first measurement was 30.96 cycles/byte and I did
not report it as the constraint** — 21 of those 31 cycles were a 16-bit loop counter living in memory.
The restructure is measured separately below.

#### 3.3 The residency policy, and why a stack rather than a cache

★★★ **This is the dispatch's "your call, and say why", and the answer comes from a measurement one task
old.** The two relevant numbers point in opposite directions:

- **LOGIC totals per title: 133–238 KB.** These say "you need a cache with eviction".
- **LOGIC working set: 4,679–8,537 bytes at a maximum call depth of 3.** These say "you need a stack".

**The working set governs.** A LOGIC resource is live exactly while the logic that opened it is running,
and `new.room`/`call` nest — that is a stack discipline by construction. **An LRU would be machinery
with nothing to evict**: at depth ≤3 nothing is ever a victim, and the eviction policy would be dead
code carrying the risk of returning a live resource's space to a new caller.

**The policy, stated as AC-5 requires:**

> **A bump allocator over a 12 KB arena at `$3000`–`$5FFF`, with a mark stack of depth 8.** `res_open`
> allocates at the top and pushes the previous top; `res_close` pops and the space above is free again.
> **There is no eviction and no victim selection — `close` IS the whole policy.**

**When it is exhausted:** `res_open` **refuses**. It sets `RES_E_FULL`, **copies nothing**, and **does
not push** — depth is unchanged, so every resource already resident stays valid at the address its
holder was given. ★★★ **This is the part that was designed against `silent wrong bytes`:** an allocator
that wrapped, or that reused a level still held, would return a plausible resource belonging to
something else, and **no byte gate downstream could see it** because the bytes would be a real
resource's bytes. **The fit test therefore runs BEFORE the copy, not after** — the first version checked
the length after `res_fetch` returned, which is a bounds check performed after the overrun.

★ **`RES_E_BIG` and `RES_E_FULL` are kept distinct**: at depth 0 a refusal means the resource does not
fit the arena at all (the largest in the corpus is 10,428 B against 12,288); above depth 0 it means the
levels already held left too little. **They are different facts and they need different fixes.**

#### 3.4 The MMU window, and the new register owner

★★ **`$FFA6` has a new sanctioned owner and it is declared, not incidental.** §2N gives the HAL
`$FF90`–`$FF9F` and `$FFB0`–`$FFBF`; the MMU task slots at `$FFA0`–`$FFAF` are **inside §2N's scan
window and owned by nobody**, which §2N notes is exactly where the siblings' real contention lives.
There is no HAL mapping API — `HAL_gfx_set_mode` remaps `$FFA4`–`$FFA7` for the framebuffer and nothing
else exists — so storage takes `$FFA6`.

**Under §2N's four-part rule, `res_core.s` contains exactly ONE register access:** `sta RES_MMU_SLOT` at
`res_core.s:173`, inside `res_map_block`, which is the only routine that writes it. The `equ` at line 41
is a definition and does not count. ★★ **`res_map_block` skips the write when the block is already
mapped**, which is why AC-7's remap count measures *real remaps* and not the call rate.

★★★ **When storage moves from `src/harness/` to `src/engine/`, `storage owns $FFA6` is the owner row
§2N.1's ratchet needs.** It cannot be added yet — the ratchet lands at first engine source and there is
none — but the fact is recorded here so it is not rediscovered.

★ **Note the collision risk this creates and that nothing currently checks:** `HAL_gfx_set_mode` also
writes `$FFA4`–`$FFA7`. **In this harness no graphics mode is ever selected, so the contention is
latent.** The phase-pair discipline of §2R.1 is the mechanism that must resolve it, and **it is not
exercised by this task.**

raw bytes with `decrypt_strings=True`. **The oracle does the splitting on both sides, so a difference
can only come from the decode.** ★ Had this script split the guest's buffer with its own parser, a
matching bug in both parsers would agree and report a pass — §2O.1 again.

★ **§2P and AC-9's own rule: no message content is printed, written or logged anywhere.** The comparison
is by count, by length, and by SHA-256 over `(index, lengths, bytes)`. **The digests are what make it
checkable without the text.** ★ The guest's own message count is compared separately, so a decode
failure localises to the split rather than to the XOR.

**AC-10 [class: suite] — candidates.** ✅ Four, §10.
