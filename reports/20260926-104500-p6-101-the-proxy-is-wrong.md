### 1 — Summary

★★★★★ **Jay's eye was right and the instrument was wrong.** `sierra_events.py`'s events/second
coalesces consecutive changed frames into one event; its own header calls it *"a lower bound on
anything per-object"* and P6.91's report said it *"must never be quoted as an equality"*. **It was
then used as Sierra's per-second rate in eight consecutive dispatches.**

★★★★★ **The non-coalescing observable is the MODAL INTERVAL BETWEEN VISUAL UPDATES, in frames** —
and because coalescing can only *lengthen* an apparent gap, the mode is an **upper bound on their
update interval** and therefore a **lower bound on their rate**:

| window | modal gap | updates/s | CPU cycles |
|---|---|---|---|
| P6.94 pen walking | **4 frames** | **15.0** | 119,318 |
| P6.94 pen standing | **4 frames** | **15.0** | 119,318 |
| P6.94 starting room | **3 frames** | **20.0** | 89,489 |
| P6.91 walking (the 5.87 window) | **3 frames** | **20.0** | 89,489 |
| Jay's run, 2026-09-26 | **3 frames** | **20.0** | 89,489 |

★★★★ **52–62% of all event gaps in the pen are multiples of 4**, and the 8/12/16-frame gaps are
cycles the 16×10 lattice **missed** — which is precisely why the event rate read **5.23** for a
window whose true cadence is **15**.

★★★★★ **Ours, for comparison, is frame-quantised too**: the medians 0.2336 / 0.1836 s are exactly
**14.0 / 11.0 frames**. So the ratio is in the same units:

> ★★★★★ **Their screen updates every 3–4 frames. Ours every 11–14. The ratio is 2.8× to 4.7×,
> centred near 3.5× — not the 5.23-against-4.4 (1.2×) the record has carried.**

★★★★★ **AND THE RATIO CANNOT BE IN THE DRAWING PATH.** Their whole cycle is **89,489–119,318 CPU
cycles**. **Our `interpret` stage alone is 156,784.** ★★★★ **Even with free drawing we would still be
slower than them** — and `interpret` is 37.5% of a cycle and **has never been profiled**, flagged at
P6.97 §7(3) and P6.99 §8(5) and not taken.

### 3 — Reasoning

**§3(2) — could the existing data answer it? YES, and no operator run was needed.** ★★★★ P6.96 and
P6.97 both found data already recorded; this is the third time. The three recordings in hand —
`sierra_pen` (P6.94), `sierra_bench_live` (P6.91) and `sierra_kq3_run` (Jay's, 2026-09-26) — carry
`changed` per frame, which is all the modal-interval measurement needs.

**§3(3) — can the lattice see a blink? NO, and that is why §4A took a different route.** 16×10 over
640×239 is **one sample per 40×24 pixels**; a 13-px-wide chicken can sit between samples entirely.
★★★★ **`changed` is a COUNT, not a pixel value**, so it cannot report *absence* — only *difference*.
★★★ A blink of N frames would appear as two changes N frames apart, indistinguishable from two
unrelated updates. ★★ §6's first trigger therefore applied to the *blink-in-frames* form, and the
task used the **interval** observable instead, **which needs no definition of absence at all.**

**§3(4) — how our own blink is bounded.** The erase and the redraw both happen inside stage 9, which
the write-tapped markers time exactly: **composite 0.12141 s = 7.3 frames** of a 14.0-frame cycle
[P6.100]. ★★★ So our sprite is absent for **some part of up to 7.3 frames**; theirs must complete
inside a 3–4 frame cycle and its events span **1–2 frames**. ★★ That is §1.2 confirmed in direction
— their erase-and-redraw is inside 1–2 frames, not demonstrably inside *one*.

**§3(1)** — nine arms at P6.100's figures; this task changed no `src/`.

**Why the mode and not the mean.** ★★★★★ The mean gap in the pen's walking window is ~11.5 frames and
the **mode is 4**. For a periodic signal observed through a lossy sampler the mode is the period and
the longer gaps are missed cycles; the mean is the period divided by the catch rate. ★★★ **Reading
the mean would have reproduced the same error in a new form.**

**Authority tier.** The cadences are **tier 2**, observed from outside. ★★★★ **Jay's eye is tier 1 and
it is what prompted the task** — the numbers were self-consistent and wrong, and only the human
noticed.

**§2H's three checks.** (1) *A second mechanism for a different object class?* ★★★★★ **Yes — and it is
the finding**: the event *rate* and the event *interval* are two mechanisms in the same data, and the
rate is destroyed by coalescing while the interval is only lengthened by it. (2) *The calling
routine.* The gaps were measured between event **starts**, not between changed frames — so a
multi-frame update counts once, which is what makes the interval comparable to a cycle. (3) *Grep the
reports.* P6.91 (the caveat), P6.94 (the ~3×), P6.96, P6.97, P6.99 — **five reports carry the figure
and P6.91's own words forbid the use the other four made of it.**
