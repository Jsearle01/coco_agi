### 3 — Reasoning

**3.1 Stage 0 first, and it is what makes the rest legible (§2).** The control — a mid-frame **palette**
change, a trick not in doubt on silicon — passed before HRES was touched. ★★★★★ **This mattered more
than anticipated: stage 1's arm then looked broken for eight experiments, and without a passing
control that would have read as "MAME cannot do mid-frame register changes" and closed 160-wide for
entirely the wrong reason.** The control is the only reason the failure was legible as an instrument
problem.

**3.2 §2H's three checks, applied to the mechanism.** (1) *A second mechanism for a different object
class?* **Yes, and it is the answer.** `$FF99` has two distinct behaviours: at **write time** only
bits 5–6 (LPF) act, so an HRES change triggers nothing; at **scanline-record time** the whole register
is captured per scanline. A first-mechanism read of the write handler alone says "HRES changes are
ignored", which is false. (2) *Name the calling routine.* The write handler is not the caller that
matters — `record_full_body_scanline()` is, and it is driven by the raster, not by the CPU write.
(3) *Grep prior reports for the same subsystem.* Done; `gfx.s` and `hal.inc` supplied every register
value and ordering constraint used, and **§2's own note that Sock's reference "documents nothing about
HRES mid-frame" was correct** — this spike supplies it for the model.

**3.3 Authority tier.** Everything here is **tier: the emulator and its source** — below Jay and below
the running original. ★★★ **A MAME pass is "worth building on", never "proven"** (§3.4): two GIME
revisions exist, the later changed video timings, and MAME models one behaviour. **No claim about
silicon is made or implied.**

**3.4 The mechanism, from MAME's source** [`src/mame/trs/gime.cpp`, fetched from mamedev/mame master]:

```cpp
case 0x09:   //  $FF99 Video Resolution Register   (bits 5-6 LPF, bits 2-4 HRES, bits 0-1 CRES)
    if (xorval & 0x60)            // xorval = old ^ new; 0x60 is LPF only
        update_geometry();
    break;
...
update_value(&m_scanlines[physical_scanline].m_ff99_value, m_gime_registers[0x09]);
update_value(&m_scanlines[physical_scanline].m_ff98_value, m_gime_registers[0x08]);
...
else if (scanline->m_ff98_value & 0x80)   /* GIME graphics */   else   /* GIME text */
```

★★★★★ **`$FF98` and `$FF99` are recorded PER SCANLINE and every scanline renders from its own recorded
copy — including whether it is graphics or text.** That is the whole mechanism, and it is what makes a
mid-frame HRES change work. ★★★ `update_value` acts **only when the value changes**, which is §7.3's
finding.

### 4 — Verification (AC-by-AC)

**AC-1 [eye-gated + measured] Stage 0, the control: a mid-frame palette change splits the screen.**
— Boundary tracks the poked delay, linearly:

```
dly    1200 1400 1600 1800 2000 2200 2400 2600 2800 3000 3200 3400 3600
y        39   53   67   81   95  109  123  137  152  166  180  194  208
```

**14 scanlines per 200 delay iterations = 1,600 CPU cycles / 14 = 114 cycles per scanline = 63.6 µs**
at the measured 1.789772 MHz, and 114 × 262 = 29,868 cycles/frame = **59.9 Hz**. ★★★★ **The instrument
recovered NTSC's line time (63.556 µs) and field rate without being told either** — the strongest
available evidence it measures the raster. Active display **y = 25..217 = exactly 192 lines**. Delays
0–1000 give no mid-screen boundary (the write lands in vertical blank). 17 of 19 points steady across
4 consecutive samples; **2 jittered by one scanline**, which is what a write landing on a line
boundary should do.
★★★★★ **Jay, at 99.99% speed, `dly 2400`:** *"i see top half white and bottom half blue. i think thats
what you'd expect."* — and it was, against a stated expectation. `colA=$3F` white over `colB=$09` blue.

**AC-2 [eye-gated + measured] Stage 1: a mid-frame HRES change holds.** —

```
dly                     1400 1800 2200 2600 3000 3400
first 160-wide row y=      70   90  110  150  170  210     <- tracks the delay
width ratio              2.01 2.01 2.01 2.01 2.01 2.01     <- the resolution HALVES, every time
row profile at dly=2400:  30:159 50:159 70:159 90:159 110:159 | 130:79 150:79 170:79 190:79 210:79
raw run lengths:          y=30 4,4,4,4,...   y=110 4,4,4,4,...   y=210 8,8,8,8,...
```

★★★★★ **The run lengths are the proof, not the counts.** `$0F` is indices 0,0,3,3 at 2 bpp; at
320-wide each pixel is 2 raster columns so two same-coloured pixels give **runs of 4**, and at 160-wide
each pixel is 4 columns giving **runs of 8**. **Both appear on the same frame, split at the scanline
the write landed on.** Guest liveness rose throughout (`s01_frames` 15 → 160).
★★★★★ **Jay, at 99.99% speed:** *"thats exactly what i saw"* — fine stripes above, double-width below,
against a stated expectation.
★★ Pass conditions were **both** required and both met: ratio ≈ 2.0 (says it is the *resolution*, not
the data re-phasing) **and** the boundary moving with the delay (says the *register write* placed it).

**AC-3 [measured] The mode pair and the geometry.** `$FF99 = $15` is 320×192×4, 80 B/row; `$0D` is
160×192×4, 40 B/row — HRES is bits 2–4 and selects bytes-per-row alone (101 → 80, 011 → 40), so the
pair changes **only** horizontal resolution. VOFFSET `$E800` = physical `$74000` = logical `$4000`
through MMU block `$3A`, confirmed by a full-screen stripe rendering correctly.

**AC-4 [NOT MET] Stage 2 — precision and jitter.** **Not reached.** Partial evidence only: every sweep
point reported steady across 4 frames, and 2 of 19 stage-0 delays jittered by one scanline. **That is
not minutes of stability and it is not a FIRQ-paced boundary.** §8.2.

**AC-5 [NOT MET] §3.2 — the address counter.** ★★★★★ **UNANSWERED, and it is the item the spike itself
ranked most likely to bite.** Below the switch the counter advances 40 bytes per row instead of 80, so
**where the text area's data comes from after the switch decides the entire buffer layout.** The
instrument is built and unused: `s01_fillm = 1` fills row *N* with byte value *N*, and with four
distinct palette entries the displayed byte value says which source row arrived. §8.1.

**AC-6 [scope note] The 4-colour pair was tested; the port's target is 16-colour.** `$1E` (320, 160
B/row) ↔ `$16` (160, 80 B/row) is deliberately untested: a 16-colour buffer is 30,720 B and would
cross `$8000` into ROM territory, forcing all-RAM mode and putting the 6809's vectors in RAM — and an
NMI through a garbage vector would look exactly like "stage 0 failed", the false negative §2 exists to
prevent. **The mode pair is already host-poked, so confirming there needs a buffer that fits, not a
rewrite.**

### 7 — Uncertainty flags

**7.1 ★★★★★ WITHDRAWN: VOFFSET is `$E800`, and `gfx.s` is not implicated.** An earlier report of mine
claimed the correct value was `$EA00` and that `gfx.s:206-215`'s `physical >> 3` formula was wrong by
`$1000`. **That was an artefact of a torn sampler and is not reproducible** — the value only ever
looked right as step 6 of a 7-step sweep, and set directly it fails. ★★★ **`gfx.s`'s open VOFFSET debt
(*"inferred from disassembly; NOT verified… discharge by sentinel test"*) is UNTOUCHED by this spike
and should not be recorded as discharged or as contradicted.** The proof that `$E800` is right is the
full-screen stripe at 4,4,4,4.

**7.2 Three defects in the spike's own instrumentation, all found late.**
- ★★★★ **`scr:pixel()` × 640 per row** reads a bitmap that changes between calls; results were not
  reproducible, and rows read **identically across all eight video modes**. Fixed with `scr:pixels()`
  — one atomic 611,840-byte whole-frame read, so a profile comes from one frame by construction.
- ★★★★★ **The adjudicator read the wrong artifact.** It counted distinct **column** patterns, which
  for a striped fill are legitimately zero, and printed **"RESULT: NO SPLIT"** over a row profile of
  159,159,159,159,159,79,79,79,79,79 at ratio 2.01 — **the exact opposite of what the run measured.**
  This is AD-131's defect class. Mode 1 is now adjudicated on the row profile and **ordered before**
  the column tests, because ordering was the defect.
- ★★★ **The liveness check could not fail.** It compared `results[1].frames` with
  `results[#results].frames`; on a one-value sweep those are the same row, so it read `x > x`, declared
  a running guest STALLED and voided a real run. **A liveness check a live guest cannot pass is worse
  than none.** Now measured within one sample window.

**7.3 ★★★★★ THE FINDING WITH THE WIDEST APPLICATION: a control that holds a variable constant can
delete the mechanism under test.** `update_value` acts only when the value **changes**. I tested the
hypothesis *"the bitmap only refreshes on register-value changes"* by adding `$FFB1` traffic to mode 1
**with `ColA == ColB`, so as not to introduce a visual variable** — equal colours never change, so
`update_value` never fired, and **I recorded the hypothesis as ELIMINATED after removing the very
thing it was about.** With `colA ≠ colB` stage 1 answered on the first attempt. ★★★ **An eliminated
hypothesis is only eliminated if the test could have confirmed it.** This is §2W applied to controls
rather than to gates, and it cost the bulk of the spike.

**7.4 Eight hypotheses were tested and eliminated** before the source was read: `-video none`; settle
time (twice, once per sampler); per-pixel sampling (**a real defect**); the MMU map and task register;
the VOFFSET value; palette-write traffic (**invalidly — §7.3**); the paired `std $FF98` write;
vblank-vs-mid-active timing. ★★ Recorded so they are not re-run.

**7.5 Scope of the pass.** One emulator (MAME 0.281), the **4-colour** pair, one machine
configuration, `poke` launch, no FIRQ. ★★★ **§3.4 is unchanged and is the binding caveat: a pass here
is worth building on and is not proof about silicon.**

### 8 — Follow-up candidates

1. ★★★★★ **§3.2, the address counter — the next task, and a blocker.** Where the data below the switch
   comes from decides the buffer layout. Instrument built (`s01_fillm = 1`), never run. **No layout
   decision should be taken before this.**
2. ★★★★ **Stage 2 — FIRQ off the horizontal-border interrupt**, boundary stability over minutes, and
   whether the handler leaves useful CPU time. §4's three questions.
3. ★★★★ **The 16-colour pair `$1E`/`$16`**, which needs a 30,720-byte buffer that crosses `$8000`
   (all-RAM, vectors in RAM — see §6.4).
4. ★★★★★ **THE STRATEGIC CONNECTION THE ORCHESTRATOR SHOULD WEIGH, and my arithmetic is UNVERIFIED
   (§8's rule):** §1 prices the 160-wide win at **47.5 cycles/pixel, 24% of the blit**, plus halved
   clear, present, restore and fill spans. T-P0-157 measured **`composite` at 59.4% of a STEADY
   cycle** — the larger half of the problem, and larger than `interpret`. **24% of 59.4% is ≈14% of a
   cycle from the blit alone**, before the other four. ★★★ **That is an order of magnitude more than
   anything the interpreter work has yielded** (P6.102 took 3.85% of a cycle across four changes;
   P6.103 took 0.387%). **This spike's subject is now on the critical path in a way it was not when
   the spike was written.**
5. ★★★ **The map ruling gains a much larger payoff** (§7 of the spike): at 160 physical the visual
   plane is 13,440 B = 1.64 slices and fits inside **two adjacent 8 KB slots**, so plane remapping
   during a composite **stops existing** rather than being batched.
6. ★★★ **The blit must move to span writes at the same time** — at 160 an isolated pixel is a
   read-modify-write, and P6.89 measured opaque runs at 1.84. **These two are one task, not two.**
7. ★★ **Text is preserved by the split**: `TXT_COLS equ 40` needs 320 physical pixels at an 8-pixel
   font, and the split gives 320 for the text rows and 160 for the picture rows. **Neither has to
   give** — and that is now demonstrated rather than assumed.
8. ★ **`fix_mojibake.py` remains red on a clean tree with a destructive repair** [T-P0-157 §4F],
   presented to Jay and **not yet ruled on**. Unrelated to this spike; carried so it is not lost.
