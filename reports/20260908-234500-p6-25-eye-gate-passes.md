### 3 — Reasoning

**3.1 ★★★★★ The "phantom" empty line was a real keypress, and the defect was mine.**

Every line after the first was submitted empty. Across three sessions this was attributed to the
debounce latch, then to `gs_done` not being reset, then to a glitching key scan. **All three were
wrong.** The cause was a change made deliberately in P6.24: the input row was cleared not on Enter but
on the *next keystroke*, so the operator could still read the line they had entered.

★★★★ **That made the first Enter change nothing on screen.** Jay, at the keys:

> *"after i type 'look at rock' the first enter appears to be eaten then when i press enter again the
> words disappear"*

The second Enter did two things at once — supplied the keystroke the deferred clear was waiting for,
**and** submitted an empty line. **The defect and the "phantom" were one defect seen from two ends.**

★★★★★ **What settled it was a control the console log could not otherwise supply: post exactly one
Enter, headlessly. One line, no second line.** A human at the keys cannot press Enter exactly once
under observation, and **nothing in the log distinguished a key the operator pressed from a key the
probe invented** — §2W's shape precisely, in an instrument that had no way to fail in the direction
that mattered.

**3.2 ★★★★ Two instruments were removed rather than kept.**

A zero-scan counter and an inter-line timer were built while chasing the wrong story. Both returned
numbers that **cannot be reconciled with the loop's own rate**: 224 idle scans reported inside a 20 ms
window, against a scan interval of ~1.8 ms — two orders of magnitude apart. Neither was ever explained.

★★★ **They are deleted, not left in place.** §2W.3: *a diagnostic that cannot be wrong does not measure
— it testifies*; a diagnostic whose reading cannot be defended is worse than none, because the next
reader will believe it. **The correct answer came from a different experiment entirely, not from
refining either counter.**

**3.3 ★★★ Backspace: the port was right and the host binding was not.**

`hal_globals.s:231` has mapped CLEAR → `$08` since P6.24, and `gs_keypress` handles it. But
`key_bindings.lua` reads off MAME:

```
row6 mask=$02 col=1  CLEAR  <- Kbd Home
row3 mask=$20 col=5  LEFT   <- Kbd Left or Kbd Backspace
```

**The operator's Backspace presses the CoCo3 LEFT ARROW**, which decodes to `$83` and which the input
line correctly discards as a movement key. Line 3 of the live run showed 14 key events for 8 characters
— five presses arriving and being dropped — which is the signature.

★★ **LEFT cannot be given to backspace: AGI walks ego with the arrow keys while the input line is up**,
so that key is spoken for. The fix belongs on the host: the gate moves host Backspace onto CLEAR, and
**moves rather than shares it** — a shared binding would close two matrix positions at once. Jay
confirmed: *"that worked"*.

★ **Authority note (§2.1/§2):** *which* CoCo3 key AGI's backspace should be is **Jay's ruling (tier 1)**,
not a ScummVM fact. ScummVM handles `$08` and says nothing about a CoCo3 keyboard. Jay's instruction —
*"probably need to tye backspace to teh coco3 clear key since it doesnt hae a backspace"* — is what the
table already encoded, arrived at independently, and is recorded here as the authority for it.

**3.4 §2H's three checks, applied to the key path.**

1. **A second mechanism for a different object class?** Yes, and it is the one that bit: **key
   DELIVERY (host → matrix) and key DECODING (matrix → code) are different mechanisms.**
   `key_coverage.lua` proves the second at 10/10 and is blind to the first.
2. **The calling routine.** `gs_keypress`'s caller is the probe's poll loop, which owns the debounce —
   the engine's `cycleInnerLoopActive` re-entry does not transfer (P6.21 §3). **The debounce is the
   caller's fact, and that is why it was searched for the defect and why the defect was not there.**
3. **Grep the reports for the same subsystem.** P6.23 concluded key delivery was at fault; P6.24 showed
   it was not (the probe was spinning at `ip_wait`). **P6.23's characterisation is contradicted and
   this report says so explicitly**, so the later one does not win merely by recency.

**3.5 §2S — sibling claims.** None made. `hal_sync_check.py` compared 11 files against POP3_port and
karateka_coco3 working trees and reported OK; no sibling artifact claim is relied on.

### 7 — Uncertainty flags

1. ★★★★ **One interactive line parsed differently from the same buffer.** A line entered *with
   backspaces* read back as exactly `LOOK AT ROCK` and parsed as `egon=1, word 0`, where the identical
   buffer without backspaces gives `egon=2, words 2,37`. **Not reproduced headlessly**, because
   `natkeyboard` cannot post CLEAR (it has no character mapping). `par_clean` walks to the NUL, so a
   stale tail past the terminator is ruled out by reading. **Next step is a measurement, not a theory:
   drive CLEAR by field assertion (idiom 41f, the technique `key_coverage.lua` already uses) and diff
   the cleaned buffer against the oracle's.** Until then AC-3 is PASSED for the posting arm only.
2. ★★ **The uppercase/lowercase table inversion is unresolved.** `hal_kb_lo` (unshifted) yields
   UPPERCASE and `hal_kb_hi` lowercase; MAME names its fields `"a  A"`, suggesting the reverse.
   `par_clean` lowercases, so **the parse is unaffected and only the echo shows it.** Deliberately not
   changed — it needs Jay's ruling on what a CoCo3 AGI input line should show.
3. ★ **The gate runs on the `poke` path.** Load and launch are ungated here.
