## ★★★ The correction that changes the shape of this answer

**Feature flags do not add opcode numbers. They replace the meaning of two existing ones**
[`tools/agivm/dispatch.py`, `unsupported_mutations()`]:

```
GF_AGI256    replaces opcode 0xAA (set.simple)  with a 256-colour picture load
GF_AGIMOUSE  replaces opcode 0xAB (push.script) with a mouse-state read
```
★★ **So a static census by opcode NUMBER cannot see a feature flag.** In a flagged title
`0xAA` and `0xAB` look exactly like `set.simple` and `push.script`. There is no set of
feature-gated opcodes to subtract from a total — there are two NUMBERS whose MEANING
differs in flagged titles. ★ Listing them as extra entries would overstate the remaining
work by implying opcodes that do not exist.

## ★★★ Feature-gated opcodes — the correlation is perfect

| opcode | base name | under the flag | flag | fan titles using it | of those, flagged | Sierra titles |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `0xAA` | set.simple | 256-colour picture load | GF_AGI256 | 3 | **3** | **1** |
| `0xAB` | push.script | mouse-state read | GF_AGIMOUSE | 15 | **15** | **0** |

★★★ **Every fan title using `0xAB` is `GF_AGIMOUSE`-flagged; every fan title using `0xAA`
is `GF_AGI256`-flagged. Not one exception either way.** That is what lets these be
separated from the standard count as a matter of fact rather than of assumption.

★★ **`0xAB` is counted as FEATURE-GATED**: no Sierra title uses it, and every fan title
that does is flagged — so in this library it is never `push.script`, it is the mouse read.
★ **`0xAA` remains STANDARD**: Sierra titles use it as a genuine `set.simple`, and only
its three flagged fan occurrences are the 256-colour opcode. **The same number is a
standard opcode in one population and a feature opcode in the other**, which is precisely
why a single flat total would have been wrong.

## ★★ Walk failures — and they are not walk failures

**83 LOGICs across 3 of 147 fan titles did not complete, and the two classes are
different things:**

| class | count | titles | what it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| record unreadable (`VolumeError`) | 81 | demo2, xmas | the LOGIC could not be READ; the walk never ran |
| opcode not in the v2 table | 2 | 0fb053 | a genuine walk stop |

★★★ **The distinction matters and the second number is the small one.** An unreadable
record is a resource-layer or interpreter-version property; only the second class could
indicate the census drifting. ★ `xmas` is **interpreter 0x2272** — the early version
`commands.py` names by game (*"AGI 2.272 (ddp, xmas) does NOT call moveObj"*) — and its
records do not parse under the v2 5-byte header. `0fb053` is **UNMATCHED** against the
detection table, i.e. a repack of unknown provenance.

★★ **The guard that says the walk itself is sound: 5,698 of 5,698 fan LOGICs that walked
ended EXACTLY at their bytecode length**, the same check `census_verify.py` applies to the
Sierra corpus. A desynchronised walk cannot do that.
